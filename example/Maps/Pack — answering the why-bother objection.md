# Pack — answering the why-bother objection

The same objection will arrive under every post: a packet of yeast does it faster. The author has answered it once, well. This pack turns that answer into reusable material so the next reply reuses the position instead of inventing a new one.

## Step 1: classify the comment
- **Efficiency.** "A packet is faster and cheaper." → concede entirely; the blog never claimed otherwise.
- **Reliability.** "A packet never dies in the fridge." → concede, then point at [[Refrigeration slows a starter but does not stop it]] for why a starter does not die either, just slows.
- **Flavour.** "It tastes the same." → this is the one with nothing behind it. Do not assert the difference; say it is being written up.

## Step 2: the notes to load
- [[Promises — the rules made in public]]
- [[Objection — Commercial yeast does the same job in an hour]]
- [[A starter is a population, not a pet]]
- [[Peak, not the clock, says when to bake]]
- [[Refrigeration slows a starter but does not stop it]]

## Step 3: the ask
> Draft a reply in the author's comment voice: plain, concedes what is right in the first sentence, holds the position without escalating, ends kindly. Reuse the position in the objection note; do not invent a new one. Under 150 words. Do not claim the flavour difference is established; if the comment raises it, say the author has promised to write it up with a source. If the commenter adds something new and true, say so and tell me whether it deserves a new zettel, with its *Not true when*.

## Step 4: afterwards
If the comment was a new angle, add a line to the objection note under *Still unanswered* or write a new objection note. If the commenter is likely to return, a person note. If anything was promised, a row in [[Commitments — promises made in public]].

## Worked example
Comment: "Yeast. One hour. Done. Why are you doing this to yourself?"
Notes: the objection note and [[A starter is a population, not a pet]].
Reply, from the notes: concede the hour, deny that the hour was ever the claim, name the practice as the subject, end with the packet-or-practice line.
