## Patterns

Four of the five new notes make the same move, which I had not noticed while writing them one at a time: each one takes an instruction that depends on a variable the instruction never mentions, and replaces it with something visible in the jar. The clock hides the temperature; "twice a day" hides the ratio; "throw it out when it goes grey" hides the fact that grey means hungry. That is a single claim about instruction-giving, not four claims about sourdough, and it is probably the thing the blog is actually about.

The fifth note goes the other way, and that is the week's real finding — see below.

## Synthesis

The week began as a log about feeding intervals and ended up being about a tension I did not know was there.

The thesis is signals over schedules: stop asking how long, ask whether it has peaked. It holds up well, and the best note of the week — the rubber band round the jar — is the clearest instance of it. A signal transfers between kitchens; a schedule does not, because a schedule is a number that silently assumes a temperature. Once that is said plainly, three of the other notes turn out to be the same argument applied to feeding, to hooch, and to the choice of flour.

Then there is the fridge. The part of that note readers repeat back to me is not the mechanism, it is the numbers: five days and it comes back on one feed, ten days and it needs two, twenty-four days and it needs four. Those are a schedule. They come from one kitchen, at one temperature, with one flour, and by the logic of every other note on the blog they should not transfer at all — and yet they are the most useful thing I have written, because they tell somebody what to expect.

So the thesis is too strong as stated. The honest version is not "never give numbers" but "a number is fine once it says whose it is." Which is, word for word, the second promise in [[Promises — the rules made in public]] — a rule I wrote in the first post and then treated as being about citations rather than about my own measurements. The rule was already right; I had been reading it too narrowly. That reframing costs nothing and rescues the useful part of the fridge note, and it puts the tension on [[Signals over schedules]] where the next person who notices it will find it already named.

The question I am now ready to ask: which of the blog's instructions are signals, which are labelled numbers, and which are unlabelled numbers pretending to be principles? The third category is the one that will embarrass me, and I suspect the flavour claim is the whole of it.
