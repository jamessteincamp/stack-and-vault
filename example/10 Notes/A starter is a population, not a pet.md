# A starter is a population, not a pet

## The idea
The mistake behind most dead starters is thinking of the jar as one thing with a mood. It is a population of yeasts and bacteria competing for the same flour, and everything you do to it is a change to the conditions of that competition. Feeding is not kindness; it is dilution and a fresh food supply. Warmth is not comfort; it is a change in which organisms outgrow which. Once you hold that picture, "why is it sluggish" stops being a question about the starter's feelings and becomes a question about what the population was given and at what temperature.

The on-camera line: "You are not keeping a pet. You are running a very small farm, and the animals are invisible."

## Not true when
It is a way of seeing, not an empirical claim, so it is not false so much as unhelpful in two cases: when a reader needs a concrete instruction *now* (a population model does not tell you how much flour to add), and when the starter is contaminated with something that is not part of the normal competition, where the answer is to discard it rather than adjust conditions.

## Why it matters
It is the frame under every other note in this vault. Feeding by ratio, reading the peak, and the fridge claim all follow from it.
