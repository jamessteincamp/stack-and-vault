# Hooch is a signal, not a failure

## The idea
The grey liquid that collects on a neglected starter ([[Hooch]]) is the population telling you it ran out of food a while ago. It is not spoilage and it does not mean the starter is dead. The useful response is to read it as a measurement: hooch on top means "hungry for hours," hooch stirred through and a sharp smell means "hungry for a day or more," and the recovery plan follows from that. Pouring it off or stirring it in is a matter of taste; the author stirs it in and says the loaf is slightly more sour.

The on-camera line: "It is not a warning light. It is a fuel gauge, and it is reading empty."

## Not true when
The liquid is pink, orange or fuzzy, or the smell is of something other than alcohol and vinegar, in which case it is not hooch and the starter should be discarded. The note is about the normal case and says nothing about contamination.

## Why it matters
It stops readers throwing away a live starter, which the feedback on Post 01 suggested was the most common reason for starting over.
