# Objection — Commercial yeast does the same job in an hour

**The objection.** A packet of yeast raises bread in an hour, costs almost nothing, and never dies in the fridge. Everything the blog describes — feeding, watching for the peak, reviving a neglected jar — is labour spent solving a problem that was solved a century ago. Strongest version, from [[@quickrise]]: "You have written two thousand words on keeping something alive that I can buy dead and working for fifty pence."

**The author's reply (best version so far).** Conceded the premise entirely: for rise, a packet is faster, cheaper and more reliable, and anyone who wants bread on a Tuesday should use one. Then narrowed the claim: the blog is not arguing that a starter is a better leavener; it is arguing that it is a different one, with a longer fermentation that changes the crumb and the flavour, and that the *keeping* is the point for the people reading. "If you want the loaf, buy the packet. If you want the practice, this is the practice." Post 02 feedback, 2026-09-04.

**Allies who answered it.** [[@ryecrumb]]: "Nobody keeps a starter because it is efficient." [[@tinyloaf]]: pointed out that the same objection applies to growing tomatoes.

**The move that works.** Concede rise, deny that rise was ever the claim, and name what the claim actually is. Do not defend the starter as the better tool for the objector's job; it is not.

**Zettels that answer it.** [[A starter is a population, not a pet]] (it is a practice, and the practice is the subject) · [[Peak, not the clock, says when to bake]] (what the practice consists of).

**Still unanswered.** Whether the flavour difference is real or a story bakers tell themselves. The author's reply asserts it and does not source it; a critic who pushed on that point would find nothing behind it yet.
