# Signals over schedules

## What this map is for

The blog's whole argument in one room. Every substantive note so far is a version of the same move: replace an instruction that depends on a hidden variable — a clock, a calendar, a recipe step — with something the reader can see in the jar. This map holds them together and records where they pull against each other.

## Core ideas

- [[A starter is a population, not a pet]] — the frame the rest sit on: everything you do is a change to the conditions of a competition.
- [[Feed by ratio, not by the clock]] — the clock is a proxy for ratio and temperature; use the real things.
- [[Peak, not the clock, says when to bake]] — the single instruction that transfers to a kitchen the author has never seen.
- [[Hooch is a signal, not a failure]] — read the jar as a fuel gauge rather than a warning light.
- [[Refrigeration slows a starter but does not stop it]] — cold is a brake, not a pause button.

## Open threads

- Whether the flavour difference is real, which is promised to [[@quickrise]] and has nothing behind it yet.
- [[Whole rye wakes a sluggish starter faster than white flour]] — a reader's claim, agreed with by the author on one try. Needs either a source or a demotion.

## Tensions / unresolved

- **The thesis says signals, the most quoted content is a schedule.** [[Peak, not the clock, says when to bake]] argues that numbers hide the variable that matters. But the part of [[Refrigeration slows a starter but does not stop it]] that readers actually repeat is a set of numbers — five days, ten days, twenty-four days — measured in one kitchen at one temperature, which is exactly the kind of instruction the thesis rejects. Either the recovery counts want restating as a signal ("feed until it peaks on schedule again"), or the thesis wants narrowing to say that schedules are fine once you say whose kitchen they came from. Not resolved, and the honest version is probably the second.
- **The frame cannot be wrong, which is either a strength or a dodge.** [[A starter is a population, not a pet]] carries `evidence: argument`, meaning there is nothing to cite and asking for a source is a category error. It is also the most-linked note here. [[Promises — the rules made in public]] says every number says whose it is; a reader could fairly ask why the claim that does the most work is the one exempt from the rule. The note's *Not true when* answers this partly, by naming the two cases where the frame is unhelpful rather than false. Whether that is enough is open.
