# Refrigeration slows a starter but does not stop it

## The idea
A starter in the fridge is not paused; it is running slowly. The population keeps eating, so the jar that was fed and refrigerated on Sunday is hungry by the following Sunday and starving by the one after. The practical consequence is that a starter coming out of the fridge needs one or two feeds at room temperature before it will peak on schedule, and a starter left three weeks will usually come back but will take several feeds to do so. The author's own count: five days between feeds in the fridge kept the starter reliable; ten days needed two recovery feeds; twenty-four days needed four.

The on-camera line: "Cold is a brake, not a pause button."

## Not true when
The fridge is running close to freezing, where activity effectively does stop and the recovery is different in kind; or the starter was refrigerated hungry rather than freshly fed, which the author's figures do not cover.

## Why it matters
It explains the most common reader complaint ("I took it out of the fridge and it did nothing") without any mystery, and it sets the feeding interval for a starter that is not baked with every week.
