# Whole rye wakes a sluggish starter faster than white flour

## The idea
A starter that has gone quiet after neglect recovers faster if the first feed or two use whole rye flour rather than white wheat. The claim, as made by a commenter and repeated by the author in reply, is that rye carries more of what the population needs to restart: more minerals, more enzymes, more of the bran the microbes live on. The author has tried it once and it seemed to work, which is not evidence, and says so.

The line in the feedback: "Rye brings it back in two feeds, every time." — [[@ryecrumb]]

## Not true when
The starter is dead rather than sluggish, in which case no flour will revive it; or the reader is keeping a white starter for a white loaf and does not want rye's flavour carried in. Also possibly not true at all: this is a claim about a mechanism ("more minerals, more enzymes") that neither the commenter nor the author has sourced.

## Why it matters
It is the kind of claim a vault exists to catch: plausible, repeated, useful if true, and resting on nothing. If it survives research it becomes the standard recovery instruction; if it does not, the post that repeats it needs a dated correction.
