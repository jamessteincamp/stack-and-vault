# Feed by ratio, not by the clock

## The idea
A feeding schedule ("every twelve hours") assumes a kitchen temperature the schedule-writer never states. A ratio ("one part starter to five parts flour and five parts water, by weight") makes the same starter behave predictably in any kitchen, because the ratio sets how much food the population has before it peaks, and the clock then takes care of itself: a 1:5:5 feed at 22 °C peaks in about eight hours in the author's kitchen, a 1:1:1 feed in three. The schedule was never the point; the ratio and the temperature were.

The on-camera line: "The clock is a proxy for the ratio and the temperature. Use the real things."

## Not true when
The kitchen temperature swings by more than a few degrees over a day, in which case the ratio alone does not predict the peak and the reader still has to watch the jar. Also not a claim about *which* ratio is best; that depends on how often the reader wants to feed.

## Why it matters
It replaces the most common instruction in starter advice with one that transfers between kitchens, and it is the reason [[Peak, not the clock, says when to bake]] is possible at all.
