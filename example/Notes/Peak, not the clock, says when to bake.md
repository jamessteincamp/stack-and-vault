# Peak, not the clock, says when to bake

## The idea
A starter is ready to leaven bread at or just after its peak: the point where it has risen as far as it will and the dome on top begins to flatten. Before the peak the population is still growing and the bread will be slow; well after it the food is gone and the bread will be sour and flat. The clock cannot find this point because the peak moves with temperature and ratio. The jar can: a rubber band at the starting level, and a glance at the dome.

The on-camera line: "Stop asking how long. Ask whether it has peaked."

## Not true when
The recipe deliberately uses a starter past its peak for flavour (some rye breads do), or the starter is so vigorous that the peak lasts minutes rather than an hour and the window is too narrow to catch by eye. Also silent on how long *after* the peak is too long; the author's kitchen suggests two hours, and that is one kitchen.

## Why it matters
It is the single instruction that makes a starter usable in a kitchen the author has never seen.
