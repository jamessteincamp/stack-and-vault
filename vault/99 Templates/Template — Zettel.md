## Sources
<!-- TWO KINDS, and they are not interchangeable.

     IN THE WORK is where the idea was said. It is provenance, not evidence.
     Saying it in three places does not make it three sources.

     OUTSIDE THE WORK is what would make a stranger believe it: a study, a
     dataset, a statute, a report, a named expert on the record. One
     citation note in 40 Sources/ per external claim, carrying author, year and URL.

     Set the `evidence:` field in the frontmatter to one of:
       external  — an outside source is cited and it says what this note says
       partial   — some of the claim is sourced, some is not; the gap is written down
       observed  — this rests on the owner's own experience, and says so
       argument  — a framing or an aphorism, not an empirical claim. Nothing to cite,
                   and asking it for a source is a category error
       asserted  — an empirical claim made with nothing behind it yet. Not a
                   failure, a commissioning brief for the next research session.

     Three tests before you write `external`:
       1. Does the source say what this note says, or something adjacent? If adjacent,
          write the gap into the citation note and use `partial`.
       2. Is the sample the population you are talking about? If not, keep the
          source and narrow the claim.
       3. Would the claim survive if the number were wrong? If not, the number is
          load-bearing and cannot stay `asserted`.

     Lived experience is a legitimate source when it is labelled as one.
     "Own kitchen, winter 2025" is a citation. "Studies show" is not. -->
