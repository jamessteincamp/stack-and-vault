# Week — how to synthesise

The daily note records what happened. The weekly synthesis is where a week of separate notes becomes thinking. Without it the daily notes are expensive bookmarks, and the vault is an archive rather than a practice.

**One hour, once a week.** Use `99 Templates/Template — Weekly synthesis.md` and write it into `30 Daily/` as `Week of YYYY-MM-DD`.

## The two questions that drive it

1. **What pattern is showing up across more than one note this week?** Two notes that turn out to be the same note, or two that turn out to be in tension, are both findings. The tension is the more valuable of the two: put it on the relevant map under *Tensions*.
2. **What did I believe on Monday that I would state differently now?** Be specific, and say which note needs rewriting. A week that revises nothing is either a quiet week or a week of collecting rather than thinking.

## Why this and not a longer daily note

The daily note is a log: it is written in the same frame of mind as the work it records, which is exactly the frame that cannot see a pattern across a week. The synthesis is written cold, from the list, and that is where the connections that matter turn up. Three hundred to six hundred words is enough. Do not summarise what each note said; connect them, and end with the question you are now ready to ask.

## What comes out of it

A synthesis that produces nothing but prose has not finished. Every one should leave behind at least one of these, and usually all four:

- **New or rewritten notes.** Written now, not queued.
- **A map updated.** New links, or a new line under *Tensions*.
- **A line in the research queue** in [[Sources — index]], if the week's thinking leaned on a claim that nothing backs.
- **One to three questions pulled forward** into next week.

## Where this sits in the cadence

Daily notes log; the weekly synthesis connects; and if you also keep a teammate stack, its audit is quarterly and asks whether any of this is still working (see [[Working with the teammate stack]]). Three cadences, each doing what the others cannot. The weekly one is the one most often skipped and the one that does most of the compounding, so it is worth protecting.
