# Why three layers

## The failure that starts it

Someone has a body of thinking: a run of essays, a series of talks, a channel, a book in progress, years of notes. They start using an AI assistant to help draft the next piece, and the results are worse than they expected. The planning documents are long, they overlap, and in places they contradict each other. The assistant, asked about one idea, answers with a blur of all of them.

The instinct is to give it more: the whole folder, every draft, the full history. That makes it worse. A model working from forty ideas at once gives a forty-idea answer to a one-idea question, and charges for every word it was sent.

The fix is not a better prompt. It is granularity. The thinking has to be held as separate claims, each precise enough to hand over on its own, each carrying what it rests on and where it stops. Then a brief is five to twelve claims chosen for the job, not the whole archive.

## The three layers

**The vault** is the Zettelkasten: the claims. One idea per note, the title stated as the claim, and five sections that force the note to be a claim rather than a topic: the idea in your own words, the condition under which it would be false, why it matters, what it connects to, and where it came from. The *Not true when* section is the one that earns the method its keep. It is the boundary of the claim, and it is what stops an assistant applying a good idea in the wrong place.

**The wiki layer** is the hub pages on top of the vault: a Start Here page, an index of objections, an index of sources ranked by weight, a ledger of promises, hub notes for each series, and pre-assembled briefing packs for the jobs that recur. A Zettelkasten without a wiki layer is a bag of five hundred good notes with no way in. The wiki layer is how a person, or an assistant, finds the right five.

**The stack** is the standing brief about the person: who they are, how they sound, how they want to be worked with, what the assistant may touch. It is small, it is loaded whole at the start of every session, and it changes slowly. Without it, every session starts by re-explaining yourself, and the assistant never learns that a compliment before a critique is a tell that the critique has been softened.

## Why they have to be separate

Each layer changes at a different rate and is loaded a different way, and that is the whole argument for keeping them apart.

The stack changes over months and is loaded whole. The vault changes daily and is loaded selectively. The wiki layer changes when the vault does and is the entry point to it. Put the stack's rules into the vault's notes and they get lost among five hundred files. Put the vault's facts into the stack and the stack swells into a stale second copy of the ledgers. `docs/the-contract.md` is the set of rules that keeps each layer doing its own job.

## What the method is not

It is not a productivity system, and it has no opinion about task management. It is not a note-taking app; it is plain Markdown and works in any editor. It is not a way to have the assistant do the thinking. The synthesis that happens in the assistant's fingers does not compound in your head; the vault is where the thinking compounds, and the assistant is an accelerator you can do without for an afternoon.

## Two things it buys that nothing else does

**Cost.** A pack of ten notes is about two thousand words. A long document is thirty thousand. You pay for what you send, so a session built on packs costs a fraction of one built on documents, and leaves room for a long working session instead of one question.

**Independence.** Everything is plain text on your own disk. No database, no account, nothing to log into. When the connection drops, the vault is still there to read, search, and write in by hand. The notes are the work; the assistant is optional.
